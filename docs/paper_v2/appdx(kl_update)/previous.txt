\subsection{Why multi-task selection benefits from a structural anchor}
\label{sec:method-motivation}

RL-based dynamic selectors that follow the standard PPO
actor--critic recipe~\cite{schulman2017ppo, schulman2016gae}
compose two scalar signals per candidate $x_i$---a difficulty term
$\mathcal{L}_i$ (loss-based; the response-token cross-entropy of
the current model on $x_i$) and an uncertainty term $\mathcal{H}_i$
(entropy-based; the mean predictive entropy over response tokens
on $x_i$)---into a single composite reward
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_i \;=\;
  w \, \mathcal{L}_i + (1 - w)\, \mathcal{H}_i,
  \label{eq:composite}
\end{equation}
with a variance-ratio weight
\begin{equation}
  w \;=\;
  \frac{\mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{L})}
       {\mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{L}) +
        \mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{H}) + \varepsilon},
  \label{eq:w}
\end{equation}
where each $\mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\cdot)$ is the
empirical variance taken \emph{over the entire candidate pool}
$\mathcal{D}$ at the current selection epoch. The construction is
adopted directly from recent IT-data-selection
work~\cite{sun2025dots, kung2023activeit}; the dataset-level
specification of $w$ is treated separately in
Sec.~\ref{sec:method-implementation}.

\paragraph{The multi-task variance trap.}
By the law of total variance, decomposing $\mathcal{L}$ over the
$M$ tasks $\{T_1, \ldots, T_M\}$ that compose $\mathcal{D}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{L})
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{m}\!\bigl[\mathrm{Var}_{x \in T_m}(\mathcal{L})\bigr]}_{\text{within-task}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}_{m}\!\bigl[\mathbb{E}_{x \in T_m}(\mathcal{L})\bigr]}_{\text{between-task}},
  \label{eq:total-variance}
\end{equation}
and analogously for $\mathcal{H}$. In a single-task pool the
between-task term vanishes, leaving $w$ as a stable summary of
within-task informativeness. In a multi-task pool with
capabilities of disparate difficulty (knowledge vs.\ reasoning
vs.\ code vs.\ multilingual), the between-task term
\emph{dominates}: it depends on the task proportions in
$\mathcal{D}$, on the relative scales of $\mathcal{L}$ and
$\mathcal{H}$ across capabilities, and on the noise in estimating
each per-task mean
$\mathbb{E}_{x \in T_m}(\cdot)$. Consequently~$w$ becomes a
\emph{sensitive function of the task-mix}, drifting between runs
at fixed hyperparameters, and the scalar reward~$\mathcal{R}_i$
can no longer separate samples by \emph{which capability} they
exercise.

\paragraph{A bounded, pool-level structural signal absorbs the drift.}
The trajectory anchor $v_l^{(t)}$ is, by construction
(Eq.~\ref{eq:anchor-def}), a pool-level summary: it is the top
principal direction of $\{\Delta h_l(x; \theta_{t-1})\}_{x \in
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t}$ across the entire probe subset, not a
per-sample statistic. Two consequences follow.

\emph{Geometrically}, $v_l^{(t)}$ captures the capability subspace
the model is most actively shaping at this epoch and layer,
\emph{independent} of how the task-mix balances within- and
between-task variance in Eq.~\ref{eq:total-variance}: the dominant
direction of hidden-state evolution is a property of the
parameter--data geometry, not of the scalar reward distribution.

\emph{Analytically}, the anchor's epoch-to-epoch change is
\emph{uniformly bounded} by the learning rate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}):
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|v_l^{(t+1)} - v_l^{(t)}\bigr\|
  \;\le\;
  \frac{2 C_\Sigma}{\gamma_{\min}}\,\eta_t,
  \label{eq:anchor-preview}
\end{equation}
in contrast to $w$, which has no such bound. The selection score
(Eq.~\ref{eq:score}) thus combines the multi-task-drifting
baseline signal $\mathcal{R}_i \cdot a_i$ with the
stability-controlled structural signal
$\widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}$, each at the variance scale
where it is most informative; the multiplicative form transfers
the boundedness of the latter to the score itself
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method-score}, App.~\ref{app:score-stability}).

\subsection{Trajectory anchor extraction}
\label{sec:anchor}

The trajectory anchor at epoch~$t$ is a per-layer set of unit
directions $\{v_l^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^d\}_{l=1}^{L}$ extracted
entirely from the parameters $\theta_{t-1}$ entering epoch~$t$ and
from the candidate pool itself. Figure~\ref{fig:anchor} illustrates
the construction. Two trajectories are at play: a
\emph{sequence-level} trajectory inside each probe sample, encoded
in the contextualisation delta $\Delta h_l$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:delta},
and an \emph{epoch-level} trajectory of the anchor itself across
training, whose stability we quantify in
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}.

\paragraph{Probe subset.}
At the start of epoch~$t$ we sample a probe subset
$\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t \subset \mathcal{D}$ uniformly at random
with $|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t| = 1{,}024$. The probe is
re-sampled each epoch with an epoch-dependent random seed so that
no fixed subset biases the anchor across the training horizon.

\paragraph{Centred covariance.}
For each layer $l$ we collect the deltas
$\{\Delta h_l(x;\theta_{t-1}) : x \in \widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t\}$
and form their mean and centred covariance:
\begin{align}
  \bar{\Delta h}_l^{(t)}
   &:= \frac{1}{|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t|}
      \sum_{x \in \widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t} \Delta h_l(x;\theta_{t-1}),
   \label{eq:mu} \\[2pt]
  \Sigma_l^{(t)}
   &:= \frac{1}{|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t|}
      \sum_{x \in \widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t}
      \bigl(\Delta h_l(x;\theta_{t-1}) - \bar{\Delta h}_l^{(t)}\bigr)
      \bigl(\Delta h_l(x;\theta_{t-1}) - \bar{\Delta h}_l^{(t)}\bigr)^{\!\top}.
   \label{eq:sigma}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Anchor as top principal direction.}
The trajectory anchor at epoch~$t$ for layer $l$ is the top unit
eigenvector of $\Sigma_l^{(t)}$,
\begin{equation}
  v_l^{(t)} \;=\;
  \arg\max_{\|v\| = 1}\; v^{\!\top} \Sigma_l^{(t)}\, v,
  \label{eq:anchor-def}
\end{equation}
sign-calibrated so that
$\langle v_l^{(t)},\, \bar{\Delta h}_l^{(t)} \rangle > 0$.
\noindent This sign calibration is not cosmetic: it eliminates
the PCA $\pm v$ ambiguity---which would otherwise let
$v_l^{(t+1)}$ flip sign relative to $v_l^{(t)}$ even when the
underlying subspace is identical---and serves as Assumption~3 of
the stability bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}.
Intuitively, $v_l^{(t)}$ is the single direction in $\mathbb{R}^d$
that best explains the spread of how layer $l$ responds to the
probe samples at the current parameter state; we read it as the
\emph{capability direction} that the model is most actively
shaping at this epoch and layer. The anchor depends only on
$\theta_{t-1}$ and on the candidate pool---no in-domain seed, no
labels, and no auxiliary model are required.

\paragraph{Alignment of candidates.}
For each candidate $x_i$ at epoch~$t$, the per-layer alignment is
the projection of the sequence-mean activation
(Eq.~\ref{eq:meanact}) onto the layer-$l$ anchor,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{align}_l^{(t)}(x_i)
  \;:=\;
  \bigl\langle \bar h_l(x_i;\theta_{t-1}),\, v_l^{(t)} \bigr\rangle,
  \label{eq:align-perlayer}
\end{equation}
and the candidate's aggregate alignment averages across all $L$
layers,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{align}_i^{(t)}
  \;:=\;
  \frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}
    \mathrm{align}_l^{(t)}(x_i),
  \label{eq:align-raw}
\end{equation}
followed by per-epoch min--max normalisation across the pool,
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\mathrm{align}_i^{(t)} - \min_j \mathrm{align}_j^{(t)}}
       {\max_j \mathrm{align}_j^{(t)} - \min_j \mathrm{align}_j^{(t)}}
  \;\in\; [0, 1].
  \label{eq:align}
\end{equation}
Averaging $\bar h_l$ over all valid positions makes the alignment
score padding-invariant and reduces dependence on any single
token's representation, matching the scoring kernel used by
NAIT~\citep{nait2026}; we report a layer-subset ablation in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}.

\subsection{Selection score}
\label{sec:method-score}

For each candidate, the selector provides the composite reward
$\mathcal{R}_i$ of Eqs.~\ref{eq:composite}--\ref{eq:w} and an
action $a_i \in [0, 1]$ from a PPO actor. Our final selection
score at epoch~$t$ is

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\,s_i^{(t)} \;=\;
\underbrace{\mathcal{R}_i \cdot a_i}_{\substack{B_i^{(t)} \,:\, \text{baseline factor}\\[1pt] \text{(high-variance, sample-level)}}}
\;\cdot\;
\underbrace{\bigl(1 + \lambda \cdot \widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}\bigr)}_{\substack{A_i^{(t)} \,\in\, [1,\,1+\lambda] \,:\, \text{anchor factor}\\[1pt] \text{(low-variance, pool-level)}}}\,
}
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}

with a single hyperparameter $\lambda \ge 0$. The multiplicative
factorisation $s_i^{(t)} = B_i^{(t)} \cdot A_i^{(t)}$ is deliberate
and carries three properties simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Clean ablation.} $\lambda = 0$ collapses
      $A_i^{(t)} \equiv 1$, so $s_i^{(t)}$ recovers the RL+CR
      baseline score exactly; any gain at $\lambda > 0$ is
      attributable to the trajectory anchor alone.

\item \emph{Variance-scale decoupling.} $B_i^{(t)}$ inherits the
      multi-task drift of Eq.~\ref{eq:total-variance};
      $A_i^{(t)}$, derived from a $1{,}024$-sample probe PCA, does
      not. Each factor operates at its informative scale---a
      separation that additive composition,
      $B_i^{(t)} + \lambda \widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}$,
      would forfeit by forcing both signals onto the same additive
      scale.

\item \emph{Stability transfer.} By the telescoping identity
      $s^{(t+1)} - s^{(t)} = B^{(t+1)}(A^{(t+1)} - A^{(t)})
      + (B^{(t+1)} - B^{(t)}) A^{(t)}$, the anchor-induced
      perturbation is bounded by
      \begin{equation}
        \bigl|B_i^{(t+1)}\bigl(A_i^{(t+1)} - A_i^{(t)}\bigr)\bigr|
        \;\le\;
        C_{\mathrm{score}} \cdot \lambda \cdot \eta_t,
        \label{eq:score-telescope}
      \end{equation}
      where $C_{\mathrm{score}}$ depends only on bounded
      quantities ($B_{\max}$, $H_{\max}$, $L_{\mathrm{norm}}$,
      $C_\Sigma$, $\gamma_{\min}$) and is independent of the
      task-mix. The proof, which combines Cauchy--Schwarz on
      $\langle \bar h_l(x_i;\theta_{t-1}), v_l^{(t+1)} - v_l^{(t)}\rangle$
      with the Lipschitz property of the min--max normalisation
      and Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}, is deferred to
      App.~\ref{app:score-stability}
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:score-stability}).
\end{enumerate}

Property~3 is, to our knowledge, the \emph{first formal stability
guarantee for any IT data-selection criterion}: the bounded factor
$A_i^{(t)} \in [1, 1+\lambda]$ inherits the anchor stability of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} and propagates it to the score, while
the baseline RL+CR drift (which TADS does not aim to remove) is
not amplified.

\subsection{Implementation note: dataset-level vs.\ per-batch $w$}
\label{sec:method-implementation}

The variance-ratio weight $w$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:w} is defined at the
\emph{dataset level}: the variances
$\mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{L})$ and
$\mathrm{Var}_{x \in \mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{H})$ are taken over all
$N$ candidates in a given selection epoch. We make this explicit
because a naive per-mini-batch implementation---recomputing
$\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{L})$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{H})$ inside
each batch and using the resulting $w_{\mathcal{B}}$ to weight the
batch's rewards---degenerates pathologically. With
$|\mathcal{B}| = 1$ (the common memory-efficient default when the
candidate-pool forward pass is bounded by activation memory rather
than throughput), the per-batch variance is identically zero and
$w_{\mathcal{B}} \equiv 0$ for every batch. The composite reward
then collapses to $\mathcal{R}_i = \mathcal{H}_i$, silently
removing the difficulty signal $\mathcal{L}_i$ from selection
across the entire training run.

The TADS implementation accumulates
$\{\mathcal{L}_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ and $\{\mathcal{H}_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$
across all batches of a selection epoch before computing $w$ once,
following Eq.~\ref{eq:w}. The cost is negligible---two scalars per
sample, freed from the forward pass---and the resulting $w$
recovers the adaptive behaviour intended by the variance-ratio
construction. In Sec.~\ref{sec:lambda-ablation} we further include
a $w_{\mathcal{B}}$-per-batch variant as an ablation cell, which
empirically reproduces the $\mathcal{H}$-only degenerate regime
and isolates how much of the RL+CR baseline's behaviour is in fact
an implementation artefact rather than the intended composite
reward.

\subsection{Algorithm}
\label{sec:method-algo}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Trajectory-Anchored Dynamic Selection (TADS)}
\label{alg:tads}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Pool $\mathcal{D}$, base model $f_{\theta_0}$, budget $B$,
         layers $\{1,\ldots,L\}$, weight $\lambda$, epochs $T$,
         probe size $|\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}|$
\FOR{$t = 1, \dots, T$}
  \STATE Sample probe set
         $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t \subset \mathcal{D}$ uniformly at random
  \FOR{$l = 1, \dots, L$}
    \STATE Compute contextualisation deltas
           $\{\Delta h_l(x; \theta_{t-1})\}_{x \in \widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_t}$
           \hfill (Eq.~\ref{eq:delta})
    \STATE Form the centred empirical covariance $\Sigma_l^{(t)}$
           \hfill (Eq.~\ref{eq:sigma})
    \STATE $v_l^{(t)} \leftarrow$ top-1 eigenvector of $\Sigma_l^{(t)}$,
           sign-calibrated
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE For all $i$: compute $\bar h_l(x_i;\theta_{t-1})$
         (Eq.~\ref{eq:meanact}) and aggregate
         $\widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}$
         \hfill (Eqs.~\ref{eq:align-perlayer}--\ref{eq:align})
  \STATE Run PPO actor--critic to obtain
         $\{\mathcal{L}_i, \mathcal{H}_i, a_i\}_{i=1}^N$
  \STATE Compute pool-level
         $w \leftarrow \mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{L})/
          (\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{L}) + \mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{H}) + \varepsilon)$
         over all $N$
         \hfill (Eq.~\ref{eq:w})
  \STATE Composite reward
         $\mathcal{R}_i \leftarrow w\,\mathcal{L}_i + (1-w)\,\mathcal{H}_i$
         \hfill (Eq.~\ref{eq:composite})
  \STATE Score
         $s_i^{(t)} \leftarrow
          \mathcal{R}_i \cdot a_i \cdot
          (1 + \lambda \cdot \widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)})$
         \hfill (Eq.~\ref{eq:score})
  \STATE $\mathcal{S}_t \leftarrow$ top-$B$ samples by $s_i^{(t)}$
  \STATE Fine-tune $\theta_{t-1} \to \theta_t$ on $\mathcal{S}_t$
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\theta_T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Theoretical analysis}
\label{sec:method-theory}

The trajectory anchor is constructed at each epoch from a random
$1{,}024$-sample probe and from the current parameters
$\theta_{t-1}$; a natural concern is whether this construction
yields a meaningful object or merely an artefact of the randomness.
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} formally rules out the latter: the
per-epoch anchor change is bounded by a function that decays
smoothly with the learning rate, and the anchor sequence converges
to a stationary direction over any finite training horizon. This
stability is what makes the anchor a structural signal rather than
a noisy summary statistic, and---via the multiplicative form of
the selection score (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-score})---it is what
gives TADS the first formal stability guarantee for any
IT data-selection criterion.

\begin{theorem}[Anchor stability]
\label{thm:stability}
Let $\Sigma^{(t)} := \Sigma_l^{(t)}$ denote the empirical covariance
of hidden-state deltas at epoch~$t$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Bounded covariance change:}
        $\|\Sigma^{(t+1)} - \Sigma^{(t)}\|_F \le C_\Sigma \cdot \eta_t$.
        \\\textit{Interpretation:} parameter updates are bounded by
        the learning rate, and the hidden-state map
        $\theta \mapsto h_l^{\mathrm{last}}(\cdot; \theta)$ is
        Lipschitz, so the empirical covariance changes only slowly
        between consecutive epochs.
  \item \emph{Eigenvalue gap:}
        $\gamma^{(t)} := \lambda_1^{(t)} - \lambda_2^{(t)}
        \ge \gamma_{\min} > 0$.
        \\\textit{Interpretation:} the top principal direction is
        sharply identified relative to the second. Empirically
        $\gamma^{(t)} \ge \STATUS{924}$ across all monitored layers
        and epochs in Phase~B (Sec.~\ref{sec:thm-empirical}).
  \item \emph{Sign calibration:}
        $\langle v_l^{(t)},\, \bar{\Delta h}_l^{(t)} \rangle > 0$
        for all $t$.
        \\\textit{Interpretation:} the PCA $\pm v$ ambiguity is
        removed by orienting $v_l^{(t)}$ along the empirical mean
        delta; enforced algorithmically in
        Algorithm~\ref{alg:tads} (line~6).
\end{enumerate}
Then the sign-calibrated trajectory anchor satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\bigl\| v_l^{(t+1)} - v_l^{(t)} \bigr\|
  \;\le\;
  \frac{2\, C_\Sigma}{\gamma_{\min}}\,\cdot\,\eta_t\;}
  \label{eq:stability-bound}
\end{equation}
For any finite training horizon $T$ with
$\sum_{t=1}^{T} \eta_t < \infty$ (satisfied by any standard cosine,
linear, or constant schedule), $\{v_l^{(t)}\}_{t=1}^{T}$ is Cauchy
and converges to a stationary anchor
$v_l^\star := v_l^{(T)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Apply the Davis--Kahan $\sin\Theta$ theorem~\cite{daviskahan} to
$\Sigma^{(t)}$ and $\Sigma^{(t+1)}$ under Assumptions~1--2:
$\sin\Theta(v^{(t+1)}, v^{(t)})
\le \|\Sigma^{(t+1)} - \Sigma^{(t)}\|_F / \gamma_{\min}
\le C_\Sigma \eta_t / \gamma_{\min}$.
Sign calibration (Assumption~3) eliminates PCA's $\pm$ ambiguity
and converts the angular bound to a Euclidean distance bound via
$\|u - v\| = 2\sin(\Theta/2) \le 2\sin\Theta$. The Cauchy property
follows by telescoping over epochs with $\sum_t \eta_t < \infty$.
The full proof, with all four steps spelled out, is given in
App.~\ref{app:proof-stability}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why this matters for TADS.}
The five practical consequences of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}---reproducibility, convergence to a
stationary direction, stability transfer to the selection score
via Corollary~\ref{cor:score-stability}, the eigenvalue gap as a
runtime diagnostic, and graceful degradation when the gap
collapses---are discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}.
