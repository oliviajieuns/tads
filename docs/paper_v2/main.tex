\title{Trajectory-Anchored Dynamic Data Selection for
       Instruction Tuning}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Data selection is central to efficient instruction tuning: a
well-chosen subset can reduce compute while shaping the capabilities
acquired by the tuned model. Existing selectors rely on externally
designed or scalar proxies---quality, difficulty, uncertainty,
influence, or their combinations---whose reliability varies across
domains, model scales, and training stages. We propose \textsc{TADS}
(\textit{Trajectory-Anchored Dynamic Selection}), a model-intrinsic
framework that extracts capability directions from the model's own
hidden-state trajectory, without in-domain seeds, labels, validation
sets, or auxiliary scorers. \textsc{TADS} augments a dynamic
selection pipeline by multiplying a reward--advantage consensus base
ranking, $\mathcal{R}_i^{(t)}a_i^{(t)}$, with a trajectory-anchor
factor, $1+\lambda\,\widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}$. Here
$\widetilde{\mathrm{align}}_i^{(t)}$ measures how well a candidate
aligns with the current trajectory anchor, and $\lambda=0$ recovers
the same no-anchor consensus base. We prove that, under a positive
eigenvalue gap, the trajectory anchor changes smoothly across
training, with per-step drift bounded by the learning rate. The
guarantee concerns the structural anchor itself and is not tied to a
particular scalar reward or advantage-estimation procedure. Across model scales
from 0.5B to 14B parameters, multiple instruction-tuning datasets,
and a diverse benchmark suite, \textsc{TADS} improves over strong
static, seed-based, and dynamic baselines while adding less than
0.5\% wall-clock overhead.
\end{abstract}
